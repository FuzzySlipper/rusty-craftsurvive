WHAT IT IS: a model reference sheet for building a 3D game creature: ONE salt-monitor, a big monitor lizard built faithfully like a Komodo dragon, in exactly the colours, hide and head of the FIRST reference image's left (walking) and middle (standing) views. Ignore the first image's right-hand view, the creature rearing upright: it is NOT part of the design. About 3 m long from snout to tail tip and about 0.8 m high at the back. It stands in a NEUTRAL STANCE on all four feet, low like a real monitor: the strong bowed legs bent and sprawled a little to the sides, all four feet flat on the ground with their long curved claws, the body level and low, the neck level and the head looking straight ahead, the long tail straight back lying on the ground. Exactly the same creature in three ORTHOGRAPHIC views, no perspective, flat even lighting with no cast shadows, on a plain flat light-grey background:
- TOP HALF of the sheet: the LEFT SIDE profile, head pointing to the left, the whole creature from snout to tail tip. In this view the far legs stand EXACTLY BEHIND the near legs and are hidden by them, so only TWO legs can be seen, one fore and one hind.
- BOTTOM LEFT: the FRONT view, looking straight at its face, both forelegs apart and visible.
- BOTTOM RIGHT: the BACK view, from straight behind, the tail toward the viewer and both hind legs apart and visible.
Landscape aspect about 1536x1024. Keep the views separate with clear grey space between them; do not overlap them. All three views at the same scale, nothing cropped. The body is long, so the front and back views are small: that is correct.

SHAPE - a real monitor lizard's anatomy simplified into a few large masses: a long heavy body slung low between the legs, a long thick neck, a long narrow flat-topped head, strong bowed legs with long curved pale claws, a long muscular tail tapering to a point. Small, cold, pale eyes under a heavy brow. NOT a dinosaur, NOT upright, NOT bulky like a bear. IN THE FRONT VIEW: the head is long and NARROW, seen end-on as a small flat wedge well narrower than the shoulders, on a neck narrower than the body; the open mouth is a DOWNWARD arch, its corners lower than its middle, a cold reptile's scowl; never a wide upturned grin, never a bulldog's, toad's or crocodile's smile. From the front the jaws are only slightly parted: a thin, flat, straight slit with a few teeth, its ends turned down; the brow heavy and low over the eyes, so the face reads cold and hostile. The body seen from the front is a low, flat-bellied lizard's, not round or chubby.

THE MOUTH: the jaws stand HALF OPEN at rest, the lower jaw clearly separate from the upper with a dark gap between them, showing rows of small serrated pale teeth and the pale pink mouth inside. The tongue stays inside the mouth. NO drool.

PAINT - follow the SECOND reference image, a sheet of a giant ground sloth from the same game, only for its treatment and not its colours: a hand-painted texture at LOW RESOLUTION with crisp visible square texels, a few authored pixel-art marks and dark line work. Cracked, chalky hide, ivory and salt-white like salt bloom on dry flowstone, with faint straw-coloured bands across the back and tail, darker dust-grey on the legs. Calm surfaces with a few strong authored details, never a busy tiled pattern, and never a patchwork of flat polygon-shaped colour blocks. Flat even lighting: paint the colours of the creature, not a light direction.

PROHIBITIONS: NO text, NO letters, NO labels, NO UI, NO watermark, NO frame lines. NO people, NO scale post, NO rocks, NO other objects. EXACTLY FOUR LEGS, NO spines, horns or frills, NO tongue sticking out, NO drool. NOT rearing, NOT upright, NOT cute, NOT photoreal, NOT a perspective render, NOT anime, NOT cel shading or outlines.
