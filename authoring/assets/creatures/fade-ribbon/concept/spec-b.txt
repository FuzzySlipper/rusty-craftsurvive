THIS DESIGN - THE FADE-RIBBON, a fog foe. A long, broad, paper-thin membrane creature that swims through the air of heavy cave mist in slow folding loops, a metre or two above the floor. Take its SHAPE, COLOURS and HEAD from the second attached reference image, and its looping swim from the third: a broad thin ribbon-membrane about 5 m long, about 1 m wide at its widest a third of the way back, and as thin as a sail, so it folds, twists and curls through the air like a long strip of silk. Its front narrows smoothly into a long, narrow, pointed head like a needle-fish or a gar: a slim upper jaw and a longer, thinner lower jaw that ends in a fine spike, both lined with a row of many small, sharp, close-set teeth along the whole length of the jaws, the teeth showing past the lips. A small dark eye near the back of the jaws, and two or three dark slit openings in a row along the side behind the head, as in the second reference. Its rear widens into a broad, soft, rounded trailing end like the loose end of a scarf. The membrane is pearly off-white, slightly translucent, with a strong violet rim along both long edges of the ribbon that fades inward into the pearl, and a violet tint on the head and jaws. No fins, no limbs, no tail spike: the membrane itself is all its body.

STYLE - THE MOST IMPORTANT PART.
