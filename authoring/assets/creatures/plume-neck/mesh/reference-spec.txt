About 3.2 m long from snout to tail tip, the back about 0.9 m high, the head on its long neck about 1.8 m high. It stands in a NEUTRAL STANCE on ALL FOUR legs like a lizard: four strong lizard legs, the elbows and knees bent and turned a little out to the sides, all four clawed feet flat on the ground, the body level, the long neck rising forward and up in a gentle curve, the head level and looking straight ahead, the tail straight back with its tip near the ground, the tail about as long as the body and neck together, not longer. Exactly the same creature in three ORTHOGRAPHIC views, no perspective, flat even lighting with no cast shadows, on a plain flat light-grey background:
- TOP HALF of the sheet: the LEFT SIDE profile, head pointing to the left, the whole creature from snout to tail tip. In this view the far legs stand EXACTLY BEHIND the near legs and are hidden by them, so only TWO legs can be seen, one fore and one hind.
- BOTTOM LEFT: the FRONT view, looking straight at its face, both forelegs apart and visible.
- BOTTOM RIGHT: the BACK view, from straight behind, the tail toward the viewer and both hind legs apart and visible.
Landscape aspect about 1536x1024. Keep the views separate with clear grey space between them; do not overlap them. All three views at the same scale, nothing cropped.

SHAPE - a real lizard's anatomy simplified into a few large masses, NOT a dinosaur and NOT a man: a lean body slung between four LIZARD legs, each with a thick upper leg, a shorter lower leg and a long splayed clawed foot; NEVER human-like legs. A VERY LONG SLENDER NECK, its own length about 1.2 m, as long as the body from shoulders to hips, rising steeply forward from the shoulders like the FIRST reference image's: THE NECK IS THE ANIMAL'S DEFINING FEATURE, never short or thick. A small, long, narrow head with long slim jaws, NOT a heavy or blocky head. A long tapering tail.

THE RUFF, THE MOST IMPORTANT PART, drawn CLOSED and as SOLID MASSES for a 3D model: around the back and sides of the head and the upper third of the neck, a collar of TWELVE large crimson frills, each ONE THICK SOLID frond shaped like a long feathery leaf with a serrated edge, as thick as a finger at its stem, attached at its base in a ring around the neck just behind the head and LYING FLAT BACK ALONG THE NECK like a closed fan, overlapping. IN EVERY VIEW the ruff is CLOSED: in the FRONT and BACK views the frills lie pressed close against the sides of the neck, so the collar is only a little wider than the neck itself; they do NOT spread out sideways and do NOT stand up around the head. Behind the collar a LOW solid crimson crest runs down the back of the neck, along the spine and down the tail, one solid ridge. NO loose hairs, NO filaments, NO thin strands.

THE FACE: cold and hostile, never cute. The jaws stand HALF OPEN at rest, the lower jaw clearly separate from the upper with a dark gap between them, showing small sharp pale teeth and the dark red mouth inside. Small hard amber eyes under a heavy brow. The mouth line straight and turned DOWN at the corners. IN THE FRONT VIEW TOO: the head is long and narrow, seen end-on as a small wedge; the parted jaws there are a thin DOWNWARD arch, the corners of the mouth LOWER than its middle, like a scowl; never a grin, never a smile.

PAINT - follow the SECOND reference image, a sheet of a giant ground sloth from the same game, only for its treatment and not its colours: a hand-painted texture at LOW RESOLUTION with crisp visible square texels, a few authored pixel-art marks and dark line work. Smooth ivory-cream scaled skin with faint darker bands across the neck, body and tail; the frills and crest a deep crimson with darker red line work; the claws dark brown. Calm surfaces with a few strong authored details, never a busy tiled pattern, and never a patchwork of flat polygon-shaped colour blocks. Flat even lighting: paint the colours of the creature, not a light direction.
