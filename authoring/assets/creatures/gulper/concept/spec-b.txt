THIS DESIGN - THE GULPER, a fog foe. A creature that is almost all mouth, hanging still in heavy cave mist a metre or two above the floor and waiting; when something passes close, its enormous jaw opens for one huge bite. Take its SHAPE, COLOURS and MOUTH from the second attached reference image: two long, narrow, straight jaw rails about 2.5 m long, hinged together at the back, that open into a vast triangular gape; both rails lined along their whole length with long, thin, uneven needle teeth, some very long and some short, pointing inward across the gape; between the rails, on each side, a thin stretched cheek membrane, pale pink-violet, that folds up when the jaw closes and stretches taut as a sail when it opens; inside, a deep violet throat narrowing back into a small rounded body behind the hinge; behind the body a long thin pale tail that trails back and twists into one delicate open loop at its end, as in the reference. Total length about 4.5 m with the tail. No eyes, or at most two tiny dark pits near the hinge. No fins, no limbs. Pale pearly off-white skin on the jaw rails and body with soft grey shading, the cheek membranes pale pink-violet with fine darker lines running from the hinge out to the rails like the ribs of a fan, the throat deep violet.

IT IS A PREDATOR SEEN ONLY WHEN IT ATTACKS: angular, enormous-mouthed and extremely tooth-centric, like an abyssal gulper eel in air. It must NOT read cute, NOT a pelican, NOT a bird, NOT a crocodile, NOT a dragon, NOT a Pac-Man, NOT a tentacled monster. No lips, no tongue, no nose, no horns, no scales pattern.

STYLE - THE MOST IMPORTANT PART. Match the first attached reference image, a sheet of a giant ground sloth from the same game, and the third and fourth, the sheets of its neighbours the needle-mouth and the fade-ribbon, only for their treatment and not their shapes: a LOW-POLYGON 3D game creature with simple smooth-shaded forms from a few large masses, and a hand-painted texture at LOW RESOLUTION with crisp visible square texels (nearest-neighbour filtering), so the pearly skin, the violet throat, the fan lines of the membranes and the needle teeth read as a few authored pixel-art marks with dark line work - a carefully authored retro texture on a modern-lit model, NOT a PS1 emulation, no jitter or blur. Calm surfaces with a few strong authored details, never a tiled or busy pattern, and never a patchwork of flat polygon-shaped colour blocks. The violet is the same soft lavender-violet as its neighbours'. Do NOT copy the ink-and-wash illustration style of the second reference image: it shows the creature, the others show how to render it.

THE FIFTH ATTACHED IMAGE IS THE PREVIOUS SHEET OF THIS CREATURE - KEEP ITS JAW RAILS, ITS FAN-RIBBED CHEEK MEMBRANES, ITS THREE POSES AND ITS LAYOUT, BUT FIX THESE:
- It is drawn as hard flat polygon FACETS: make every surface SMOOTH-SHADED, rounded and soft like the needle-mouth's and the fade-ribbon's, no visible triangles or flat planes.

- Its tail is a short stub with a small ring: make the tail LONG, thin and ribbon-like, about as long as the jaw, trailing back from the body and twisting into one LARGE delicate open loop at its end, as in the second reference.
- It is too small: make the whole creature about 4.5 m long, about two and a half times the 1.8 m post, the jaw about 2.5 m of that.
- The body behind the hinge is a small round bulb: keep it small but give it a smooth swelling shape that flows from the jaw's back into the tail, as in the second reference.

WHAT IT IS: a model turnaround sheet from a game studio character viewer, for ONE creature. Three views of the SAME creature side by side, each floating in the air about 1.5 m above a plain flat warm-grey floor with its soft shadow on the floor below it, on a plain warm-grey backdrop:
