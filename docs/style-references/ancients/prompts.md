## Shared prompt

A single in-game screenshot from a first-person 3D exploration game: the inside of an ancient buried building deep underground, a room about 8 m square and 6 m tall entered from a corridor, seen at eye height (1.7 m) from the doorway. One ruined doorway or arch leads on to a darker space beyond; part of one wall has collapsed and natural cave rock pushes in through the gap.

Rendering method (follow exactly, it is how the game is actually drawn): low-poly modular architecture pieces with chunky readable silhouettes and SMOOTH shading (not flat-shaded facets); small crisp textures at about 32 texels per metre, magnified nearest so the texels read as pixel-art up close, with dark crack line work exactly one texel wide; each surface is a calm, nearly flat broad colour carrying only a couple of strong authored details (a few bold cracks, one ceramic accent), never a busy texture tiled everywhere; no photographic grain or realistic PBR detail. The cave rock in the collapse is the game's smooth voxel rock drawn as 3D pixel art: small lit facets of flat palette colour with soft strata, no painted rock picture. Look like the attached reference of the overworld ruin kit (pale worn stone, pixel cracks, teal ceramic insets) and the attached cave rock render.

Light: underground there is no ambient fill; the player's own warm lantern lights the near room, and one relic light (a small softly glowing ancient fixture, cool pale teal-white, set into the architecture) is the other source; beyond them it falls to deep cool darkness, but the floor, the doorway and the collapse stay readable. Warm lantern light against cool darkness.

Palette: worn pale stone (bone, sand, warm grey), softened teal/turquoise ceramic, a little faded red cloth; coloured shadows. No lime green, no neon, no saturated colour everywhere.

Wide 16:9, about 1536x864. NO text, NO UI, NO people, NO creatures, NO weapons, NO watermark.

WHO BUILT IT: not any Earth culture. No Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Gothic or cathedral forms (no pointed Gothic arches, no classical columns or capitals, no pharaonic lintels), not elvish fantasy, not science-fiction panels or skyscrapers. It was built thousands of years ago by a fallen civilization of fused magic and technology, of unimaginable power and whim, at the height of a 1970s cosmic-fantasy aesthetic (think the spirit of Roger Dean, Moebius and Bruce Pennington paperback covers, translated into stone): aristocratic, vintage, luxurious taste, with discordant technology present only lightly, as an odd element or two inside the grandeur. It was destroyed at its height, and what the explorer finds now, long after, are its weird bones: confusing, unplaceable, eroded to dull stone and dust. The attached first study (pale stone room with ribs, a teal mosaic roundel and a round porthole) is the right general region, but it reads too elvish; make it stranger and less earthly.

## Whim

VARIANT B2, "Whim": the room is built on impossible, whimsical proportions. The doorway onward is far too tall and narrow, a slot rising most of the wall's height and widening into a round eye at the top; an overscaled scalloped shell-like alcove; a stair rising along one wall into the vault and ending at nothing; a heavy stone sphere hangs from the vault on a single curving rib; the ceramic mosaic shows an alien sky of ringed moons in faded teal and ochre; one discordant technology element: a set of thin, perfectly straight dark rods standing in a row, untouched by the erosion, with the relic light glowing between them.

## Bones

VARIANT B3, "Bones": the grandeur is mostly gone and the room is its skeleton. Tall curved ribs rise like the ribcage of a vast animal, several broken off; the panels between them have fallen, showing cave rock behind; the floor's slabs are heaved and cracked with porcelain-like ceramic tiles scattered; a strange low plinth shaped like a reclining seat or throne of fluid curves, eroded smooth; one discordant technology element: a smooth black glassy slab set upright in the floor, perfectly intact and timeless against the eroded stone, with the relic light a cold glow inside it.
